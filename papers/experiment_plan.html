Five findings that reset the ordering

Verified on the cluster on Aug 27. The first one was found while executing Day 1 and it invalidates the previous audit — including my own, which counted directories instead of weight files.

Catastrophic

The trained models are gone, not merely at risk

Scratch runs a ~45-day rolling purge that deletes weight files but leaves the directory skeletons behind. A destroyed run still lists checkpoint-46000 / 48000 / 50000, and checkpoint-50000/PatchCrossAttnProjector_1/ still exists — as an empty directory. Any audit done with ls reports the run as healthy. Mine did.

A weight-file count tells the truth: of seven runs, six contain zero .bin/.safetensors files. V-JEPA, DINOv2, CLIP, NoJEPA-LoRA and Layers4 are all destroyed, and so is the Stage-A projector that every two-stage result depends on. Searched depot, home and all other scratch roots: no copies exist. Only ContextFluxBlurMap survived, because it is 45 days old rather than 65 — it was hours from the same fate and is now backed up to depot.

The oldest surviving weights anywhere are dated Jul 13, so the boundary is real and it keeps moving: more runs roll off through September. The encoder comparison no longer exists and cannot be evaluated — it has to be retrained or dropped.

Resolved

The paper had no adequate test set

Deblur_MIOR holds 7,849 training pairs, but the validation split is 11 paired images. val_blur (80) and test_blur (75) are blur-only, with no ground truth, so they support no-reference metrics only. Every model was trained on all 7,849 train pairs (~25 passes each), so carving a test split out of train is testing on training data.

Prior experience on this cluster put run-to-run noise near ±3 dB on an 11-image split. No encoder comparison would separate on that.

Fixed on Day 1. Regenerating MIOR-style blur was ruled out — the train pairs are named sequentially with no mapping back to source images, so disjointness cannot be proven. Instead the GoPro test split is now indexed as a flat paired set: 1,111 real blur/sharp pairs, a benchmark reviewers already know, guaranteed disjoint from MIOR training. It lives at /depot/chan129/users/harshana/eval_sets/gopro_test as symlinks (2.7 MB, zero broken links). The cost is a domain shift from synthetic MIOR blur to real camera blur — report it as such.

Design flaw

The encoder comparison is confounded

The headline V-JEPA run is the only one in the family using jepa_projector_heads: both — it feeds the backbone through two pathways (projected text tokens plus pseudo VAE-latent tokens). DINOv2 and CLIP use a single-head text-only projector by architecture, and Layers4 and GTSource both use heads: text.

So three separate comparisons were contaminated: V-JEPA vs DINO/CLIP, N=1 vs N=4 depth, and LQ vs GT source. A reviewer would have said V-JEPA won because it had an extra conditioning channel.

Since those runs no longer exist, this stops being a repair job and becomes a design rule for whatever gets retrained: hold jepa_projector_heads fixed across every encoder arm, and treat text-versus-both as its own deliberate ablation rather than an accident of configuration.

Resolved

The V-JEPA hub cache was purged, and the LQ rerun is dead

The first LQ-align attempt died instantly on FileNotFoundError: /scratch/.../hub/facebookresearch_vjepa2_main/hubconf.py — the same purge took the torch.hub repo directory. It silently re-downloaded four minutes later, which is why the second attempt got as far as it did. Fixed on Day 1: the hub repo and the 5.1 GB V-JEPA weights are copied to depot, and job scripts now export TORCH_HOME there with a fail-fast check.

The second attempt (17:29) trained 51 steps and was then SIGTERM'd at 17:49 when the interactive allocation ended. It wrote no checkpoints. The LQ-aligned projector does not exist, and at 10.4 s/it a full 20K-step Stage-A run needs 58 hours of uninterrupted GPU. Use batch jobs, not sinteractive, for anything that must survive the night.

Constraint

Compute is the binding constraint, and it is shared

Both GPU requests sit PENDING on AssocGrpGRES — the stanchan account-wide GRES cap, currently consumed by eight running jobs across five other users. This is not a personal quota you can wait out predictably.

The plan below is therefore tiered by GPU dependency rather than by importance alone: Tracks A–D are designed to finish on scraps, and only Track E needs sustained A100 time. Freeing capacity by cancelling labmates' allocations is your call to make, not something to do quietly.

The gate moved to today

The Sep 1 gate was designed around a choice between a full encoder comparison and an analysis-led fallback. The purge has already made that choice. Rebuilding even one arm costs 2.4 days of Stage A plus 3–6 days of Stage B, sequentially, on GPUs that are not currently allocated. Four arms is arithmetically impossible in thirteen days, so the paper cannot claim a controlled comparison across V-JEPA, DINOv2, CLIP and VL-JEPA.

Plan for the analysis-led paper now, not on Sep 1. Its thesis: blur-robustness of a self-supervised representation predicts its value as conditioning for a frozen generative restorer — carried by C1's dataset-scale invariance analysis and C3's linear-probe restorability across all three encoders, both of which are cheap, unaffected by the purge, and comparable across encoders without training a diffusion model per encoder. The system section then presents ContextFlux as the instantiation, with whatever arms E0 and E1 deliver as evidence rather than as the whole argument.

That reframing is not a consolation prize. An encoder comparison at N=1 per arm on 11 validation images was never going to separate anyway; a predictive analysis across hundreds of image pairs is the stronger claim, and it is the one the surviving evidence can actually support. The abstract and Section 4 should be rewritten this week, not after E1 resolves.
