Resolved

The paper had no adequate test set

Deblur_MIOR holds 7,849 training pairs, but the validation split is 11 paired images. val_blur (80) and test_blur (75) are blur-only, with no ground truth, so they support no-reference metrics only. Every model was trained on all 7,849 train pairs (~25 passes each), so carving a test split out of train is testing on training data.

Prior experience on this cluster put run-to-run noise near ±3 dB on an 11-image split. No encoder comparison would separate on that.

Fixed on Day 1. Regenerating MIOR-style blur was ruled out — the train pairs are named sequentially with no mapping back to source images, so disjointness cannot be proven. Instead the GoPro test split is now indexed as a flat paired set: 1,111 real blur/sharp pairs, a benchmark reviewers already know, guaranteed disjoint from MIOR training. It lives at /depot/chan129/users/harshana/eval_sets/gopro_test as symlinks (2.7 MB, zero broken links). The cost is a domain shift from synthetic MIOR blur to real camera blur — report it as such.

Design flaw

The encoder comparison is confounded

The headline V-JEPA run is the only one in the family using jepa_projector_heads: both — it feeds the backbone through two pathways (projected text tokens plus pseudo VAE-latent tokens). DINOv2 and CLIP use a single-head text-only projector by architecture, and Layers4 and GTSource both use heads: text.

So three separate comparisons were contaminated: V-JEPA vs DINO/CLIP, N=1 vs N=4 depth, and LQ vs GT source. A reviewer would have said V-JEPA won because it had an extra conditioning channel.

Since those runs no longer exist, this stops being a repair job and becomes a design rule for whatever gets retrained: hold jepa_projector_heads fixed across every encoder arm, and treat text-versus-both as its own deliberate ablation rather than an accident of configuration.

The headline result does not separate V-JEPA from DINOv2. On PSNR they sit 0.10 dB apart against a 1.15 dB standard deviation — statistically indistinguishable — and on SSIM the order reverses, with DINOv2 ahead on both GoPro and MIOR. The one clean separation is CLIP, roughly 3 dB behind both. That supports "text-aligned contrastive features carry less restorable structure", but it does not support "JEPA is the right encoder."

Two caveats that belong in the paper rather than in a footnote. Probe reconstructions land near 17.7 dB while the blurred input itself scores 26.3 dB, so this metric ranks encoders relatively and says nothing about absolute restoration quality. And the MIOR arm of the same experiment rests on n=3 validation images — it is directional at best, which is why only the n=50 GoPro arm is plotted here.

The per-block curve is the more quotable finding. Energy retention climbs steeply through the first ten blocks, peaks near block 10, then declines slightly — so mid-depth V-JEPA features are the most blur-robust, and the last block is not the best place to tap. That answers the depth question the two destroyed training runs were meant to answer, from features alone, in hours instead of days.

The pivot: train the encoder for the degradation

The measurements said the conditioning channel is information-limited. Rather than routing around that, train the representation itself against blur. This is the strongest move available, and the validation for it is nearly free.

Why it works

It attacks the number we actually measured

X3 is the whole argument: a frozen V-JEPA representation of the blurred image decodes to 17.7 dB against a 26.3 dB blurred-input floor. The representation is lossy because its objective made it lossy — JEPA predicts masked-region embeddings precisely so it can discard unpredictable high-frequency detail, which is the content deblurring exists to recover.

Training the encoder with a blur→sharp objective inverts that. Instead of learning what survives blur, the encoder learns what the sharp image looks like in latent space given the blurred one — which is exactly the signal a frozen generative prior needs, and exactly what the current pipeline cannot supply.

And the paper arc gets better, not worse: off-the-shelf self-supervised features are information-limited for restoration (measured, Sec. 4) → training the objective against the degradation recovers part of it (measured, same axis). A negative result that motivates a fix beats either half alone.

Design risk

A pure JEPA objective could reproduce the same problem

JEPA avoids pixel reconstruction deliberately, to get abstract features for recognition. Restoration wants the low-level detail back. So a latent-predictive objective can rediscover exactly the abstraction that caused the bottleneck — unless the asymmetry does the work: predicting the sharp target from the blurred context forces the representation to encode what differs between them.

Two failure modes to instrument from step one. Collapse — JEPA without an EMA target encoder, stop-gradient, and an asymmetric predictor converges to constant outputs with a beautiful loss curve; track feature rank and per-dimension variance, not just loss. A blur-invariant target — if the target encoder is itself insensitive to blur, the prediction task is trivial and nothing is learned. The target must retain the detail we are asking the context branch to recover.

Also: fine-tune, do not pretrain. V-JEPA was trained on millions of videos; there are 7,760 training pairs here. From scratch will underperform the frozen checkpoint and burn the window doing it.

The good part

It validates without the diffusion model

The X3 probe is already built, already run, and already has a baseline: 17.66 dB frozen, against a 26.3 dB floor. Re-running it on a deblur-trained encoder measures the improvement directly — no Stage-B, no sampling, no 53.7 s/image. If probe PSNR climbs toward or past the floor, the contribution is demonstrated on the same axis the problem was diagnosed on.

That decouples the paper's main claim from the training queue entirely. Even if no Stage-B arm lands by Sep 6, this result stands on its own.

The design critique — and what it changes

A systems read of the architecture, run against the measurements already in hand. It changes the paper's thesis and reorders what is worth running with the time left.

Measured

The trained system is worse than doing nothing

The critique predicted this; the evaluation now confirms it on real data. Scored against the blurred input on identical images with the same 30-px border crop: −1.81 dB on 300 GoPro pairs (25.67 vs 27.48) and −2.93 dB on MIOR val (25.76 vs 28.69). SSIM is lower too, on both. This is no longer a hint from a five-image split — 300 pairs at a 2.05 dB standard deviation.

The generative prior is not restoring the image, it is replacing it with a plausible one. That is the expected outcome when the observation reaches the backbone only through a semantic channel and nothing enforces y = k * x̂.

Structural

The conditioning path is information-limited, and X3 measures it

The JEPA objective predicts representations of masked regions, not pixels — it is trained to discard unpredictable high-entropy detail, which is exactly the high-frequency content deblurring exists to recover. The system asks a representation optimised for abstraction to do a codec's job.

X3 puts a number on it: the linear probe reconstructs at 17.7 dB while the blurred input itself is 26.3 dB. A linear decoder is a lower bound, but decoded naively the representation carries less usable image content than the degraded pixels would. That matters because use_condition_image_for_transformer: false makes projected V-JEPA tokens the only path from observation to backbone — the entire fidelity budget runs through the lossier channel.

A second signal agrees: BlurMap scored 24.44 dB against a MIOR blurred-input floor near 29.7 dB. Different small samples, so treat it as directional — but a model scoring below its own input is not deblurring, it is re-imagining. That is the expected failure when a generative prior is disconnected from the forward model.

Framing

Deblurring is an inverse problem; the system treats it as conditional generation

Deblurring carries a hard fidelity constraint, y = k * x + n. Nothing in this pipeline knows the blur kernel, and nothing ever checks that the output, re-blurred, reproduces the input. Frozen text-to-image prior, semantic conditioning, no degradation model, no measurement consistency — every choice treats the task as generation.

Three consequences are cheap to act on. Restore the latent path so V-JEPA carries semantics while VAE latents carry geometry — a 32×32 token grid cannot encode sub-pixel edge position across 1280×720, and a latent is built to. Add a data-consistency step at sampling, which needs no retraining at all. Re-tap mid-depth: X4 already says block 10 beats the block 23 currently used.

One self-inflicted wound worth removing: Stage A regresses V-JEPA tokens onto Qwen3-VL caption embeddings. The introduction argues text is a lossy conditioning channel, then trains the projector to imitate it. The VAE-latent head is far better motivated.

Plumbing

Two resolution bugs sit between the model and its score

Aspect distortion. jepa_helpers.py resizes with Resize((size, size)) — square. Training used 512×512 crops, so that resize was identity; at native 1280×720 inference the input is squashed 1.78× anisotropically, so the V-JEPA tokens describe a distorted image while the latent grid they steer is not. Raising vjepa_image_size does not fix this — a square resize distorts at any size.

Train/test scale. BlurMap trained at 512×512 and was scored at 1280×720 (3.5× the area) and 1920×1080 (7.9×). The packed latent sequence length changes with resolution, and calculate_shift derives the dynamic time-shift from sequence length — so the sampler ran on a different noise schedule than it trained under. A 512-crop control (job 11632229) is queued to separate this from the architectural bottleneck.

Note the resolution sweep argues against simply going bigger: cosine to the 384 px pretraining reference is 0.916 at 512 but 0.904 at 768 — non-monotonic, so more pixels is not automatically better features.

Reframing

Invariance is a role assignment, not a ranking

X4 was read as "which block is best." The sharper reading is that a blur-invariant block, by construction, carries no information about the blur. You want both: invariant mid-depth features for semantics, and blur-sensitive early features for kernel estimation. The invariance curve is the diagnostic that assigns each block a job — a better story than picking a layer, and it costs one more analysis run rather than a training run.

The gate moved to today

GPU hours are no longer the constraint — wall-clock is. More GPUs let arms run beside each other, not faster individually, so the binding limits are now: the Sep 9 freeze, the fact that Stage-B s/it has still never been measured, and code that does not exist yet. An arm started after ~Sep 3 cannot be trained and evaluated in time no matter how much hardware is behind it.

That reorders the first move. Launch one arm immediately with a randomly-initialised projector and read s/it within fifteen minutes. It needs no Stage-A, so it removes the serial dependency on X9 entirely, it sizes the step budget for every other arm, and it doubles as the "does Stage-A alignment help at all?" ablation — which has never actually been tested.

The encoder pivot outranks the arm matrix. F1 is ~15 lines against an existing training path, it targets the one number the whole analysis rests on, and F2 validates it with a probe that needs no diffusion sampling at all. The B-arms test whether the architecture wastes information; F1/F3 test whether the representation can be made to carry it. The second question is both cheaper to answer and closer to the paper's thesis — run it first, and let the arms run beside it rather than ahead of it.

Spend the remaining hardware on hypotheses, not on more encoders. The temptation is to rebuild the four-encoder comparison the purge destroyed. That answers a weaker question than the one the critique opened up. The higher-value factorial holds the encoder fixed and varies what the architecture is missing — above all latents on versus off, which measures the information bottleneck at the system level rather than through a linear probe.

The thesis should move with the evidence. "Blur-robustness predicts conditioning value" is no longer quite what the data says — X3 refused to separate V-JEPA from DINOv2, and the critique explains why that was always the wrong axis. The stronger claim the measurements actually support: what is the information budget of a self-supervised representation used as the conditioning channel of a frozen generative restorer, and what does that prior need beyond semantics? Y1 makes it a figure by putting the VAE latent and the raw degraded input on the same probe axis; X4 and Y2 split the encoder into semantic and degradation-carrying roles; Y3 shows what a measurement-consistency step buys without any training at all.

A negative result that is measured, explained and then partly repaired is a better ICASSP paper than a win claimed on eleven images. The honest arc is: semantic conditioning alone is information-limited (Y1) → here is where in the encoder the missing information lives (X4, Y2) → here is a training-free fix that recovers part of it (Y3). Write that spine now; do not wait on any training run.

VAEBlurAlign — the primary track now

Not F1–F3's "unfreeze V-JEPA inside Stage-A." A separate, more direct training path: LoRA-adapt the FLUX.2 Klein VAE's own encoder — the same latent space the transformer already consumes natively, no cross-space projector to misalign. Started 08/29 evening, still running. Supersedes the JEPA-conditioning tracks below as the paper's constructive contribution.

Why the VAE and not V-JEPA. Sec. 4's probe put a number on the problem: a frozen V-JEPA representation of a blurred image decodes to 17.7 dB against a 26.3 dB blurred-input floor — the representation is lossy because its predictive objective made it lossy, discarding exactly the high-frequency content deblurring exists to recover. The Flux VAE has no such ceiling: its own decode(encode(x)) round trip on a clean image costs just 42.4 dB (measured, GoPro probe, n=50), and a linear probe from a blurred image's VAE latents back to the sharp image already reaches 26.85 dB against a 26.86 dB floor — essentially matching "do nothing" with a linear map and zero training. That is real headroom: a nonlinearly-trained encoder has somewhere to go that V-JEPA's channel never had.

The mechanism. VAEBlurAlign_model LoRA-adapts only the encoder (decoder frozen, matching every downstream assumption). Each training sample splices one real blurred block from Deblur-MIOR into an otherwise-clean GT crop — most of the frame is clean context, one block is corrupted — mirroring how JEPA trains (context visible, target predicted) rather than blurring the whole frame and matching everywhere. Latent (and pixel) loss is computed only at the corrupted block. Two arms run head-to-head under identical settings: VAEBlurAlign.yml (patched, clean context available) vs. VAEBlurAlign_fullblur_baseline.yml (the same mechanism with replace_ratio=1.0 — the whole crop corrupted, no context at all). The open question both arms exist to answer: does clean surrounding context actually help recovery?

Seven fixes it took to get a trainable signal — condensed from the full engineering log in findings/VAEBlurAlign_findings.md:

  1. Separate frozen target VAE. A shared LoRA-adapted encoder for both branches meant the "fixed" target drifted every step because it shared live weights with the branch being trained — chasing a target it was itself moving. Fixed with a second, permanently frozen VAE copy for the target only.
  2. Real batching. Single-example gradient steps over thousands of distinct (crop, block) tasks showed ~zero population trend even though an overfit-one-sample check proved gradients were fine. FullResPatchifierDataset now loads full-res images once and draws several same-shape crops per group so they actually stack into real batches.
  3. Dropped VGG perceptual loss and the reconstruction-anchor term. Removing them exposed a regression that had been latent all along: decoded output was measurably worse than doing nothing in 11–12 of 12 validation images. The anchor had been the only thing keeping general decode fidelity honest outside the supervised region.
  4. GAN loss — tried three times, abandoned. A PatchGAN discriminator against the full decoded frame, hypothesizing checkerboard artifacts caused #3. All three attempts (default weight, reduced weight, reduced weight + 10× weaker discriminator LR) ended in discriminator collapse or no improvement over baseline — 12/12 samples worse than doing nothing regardless of D/G balance. gan_loss_weight: 0 in every current config.
  5. Root cause of "worse than baseline": a noise floor, not a training bug. The frozen, untrained VAE's own round trip costs 0.0005–0.0019 MSE on a clean image from lossy encode/decode alone. For any block whose true blur-vs-GT signal is smaller than that, beating "do nothing" is mathematically impossible — a large uncontrolled fraction of every batch was an unwinnable comparison by construction, explaining every prior "worse than baseline" result.
  6. Block-floor filtering. Reject block placements below min_block_floor_mse (0.002, with margin over the measured floor). Only partly effective alone — retrying block position within an already-blur-free crop can't manufacture blur that isn't there; 38% of selected blocks stayed below threshold.
  7. Crop-position energy biasing — the fix that mattered. Motion blur is usually localized; a uniformly random crop often misses it. A per-pixel blur-energy integral image, computed once per loaded pair, now scores every valid crop position and samples among the top-20 highest-energy ones. Verified directly: below-threshold blocks dropped 38% → 11%, and median block floor MSE roughly quadrupled (0.0028 → 0.0101).

What has to happen after the run finishes — none of this exists yet:

Re-evaluate above-floor only

The old fixed 12-sample panel is biased toward unwinnable comparisons (7 of 12 fall below the recoverability threshold even after crop-bias). Draw a fresh, larger panel now that the biasing fix is in place, and report only on samples above the noise floor.

Decide patched vs. full-frame

The ablation's actual point. Still unresolved at 41% through the run — needs the finished 6,000-step checkpoints, not the intermediate ones.

Wire it into an end-to-end deblurring eval

The critical missing piece. Every number so far is block-region latent/pixel loss, not a full deblurred-image PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS comparable to a baseline table row. Two paths: decode straight from the trained encoder with no diffusion step (the design critique's own suggested control), and/or feed it as the conditioning image into a frozen flow-matching pass. Neither is built yet.

Revisit the declined mask-channel idea

Feeding the block mask as an explicit input channel (widening conv_in 3→4) was declined because LoRA deltas can't express a structural change like that. Worth reopening if the finished run's scalar block-supervision plateaus again.

What we are not doing

All four were already marginal. With Stage A to redo before any Stage-B run can start, they are now unreachable — and saying so plainly beats leaving rows marked "pending" in a submitted paper.

Layers 2 and 8

N=1 versus N=4 already carries the depth argument. Two more full runs buy a smoother curve nobody asked for.

VL-JEPA rerun

Its checkpoint stops at 6K of 50K. A full rerun costs the same as E1 and repairs nothing that is confounded.

LQ-aligned Stage A → Stage B

58 hours of alignment before Stage B even starts. Cut it, and drop the LQ-aligned row from Table 3 rather than leaving it pending.

GT-oracle Stage B

A genuinely interesting upper bound, and the first thing to promote if the queue clears unexpectedly. Not worth displacing E1.

Reblur-consistency training

The highest-value fix in the critique — penalise ‖k̂ * x̂ − y‖ — but it is a training change, and there is no training budget left. Name it as the next step, with the cycle-loss code that already exists as the starting point.

One-step distillation

53.7 s/image is research-only, and few-step models usually win on distortion metrics for restoration. OSEDiff machinery is in the repo. Out of scope for eleven days.

Burst input, EXIF and field-varying PSF

What a camera actually offers: multi-frame makes the problem far better posed, exposure time bounds the blur length, and the DFlat/DeepLens code already models field-varying PSFs. All are the paper's future work, not its content.
