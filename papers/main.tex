\title{ContextFlux: Conditioning a Frozen Flow-Matching Transformer with Blur-Robust V-JEPA Context for Motion Deblurring}

\name{Harshana Weligama\thanks{\authnote{Funding/acknowledgment; verify ICASSP 2027 blinding policy before submission.}}}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Diffusion-based image restoration methods condition large text-to-image backbones on captions extracted from the degraded input, but captioning a blurred image is unreliable and discards spatial detail. We ask whether the patch representations of a self-supervised video encoder, V-JEPA, can replace text conditioning entirely. We first analyze V-JEPA features under motion blur and find that they remain strongly correlated with the features of the sharp image, making them a natural blur-robust conditioning signal. Building on this, we propose \emph{ContextFlux}: a lightweight dual-head cross-attention projector maps frozen V-JEPA patch tokens into (i)~the text-conditioning space and (ii)~the packed VAE-latent space of a completely frozen FLUX.2 flow-matching transformer. The projector is first aligned to caption embeddings and clean-image latents, then fine-tuned with the flow-matching objective while the 4B-parameter backbone, the VAE, and the encoder all stay frozen — the projector is the only trained component. On a synthetic motion-blur benchmark, we compare V-JEPA context against DINOv2, CLIP, and VL-JEPA conditioning, a LoRA-tuned image-conditioned control, and regression baselines, and ablate feature depth and feature source. \authnote{Finalize abstract claims once the unified evaluation completes (plan step 5).}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%=============================================================================

Recovering a sharp image from a motion-blurred observation is a long-standing inverse problem. Regression networks such as Restormer~\cite{zamir2022restormer} and NAFNet~\cite{chen2022nafnet} achieve strong distortion metrics but over-smooth fine texture. Generative restoration instead exploits the prior of a large pre-trained text-to-image model~\cite{wang2024exploiting,lin2023diffbir,wu2024osediff}, and recent flow-matching transformers~\cite{lipman2023flow,esser2024scaling,flux2024} make this prior stronger and cheaper to sample.

To steer such a backbone toward the latent clean image, current pipelines extract \emph{semantic} conditioning from the degraded input — typically a caption or tag set produced by a vision--language model, following the caption-free-restoration line of LucidFlux~\cite{lucidflux2025}\authnote{verify LucidFlux citation details}. This is doubly unsatisfying for deblurring: a VLM applied to a blurred image produces unreliable text, and even a perfect caption is a severe information bottleneck — it carries no spatial layout.

We instead condition on the degraded image's \emph{self-supervised patch representations}. V-JEPA~\cite{assran2023self,bardes2025vjepa2}\authnote{verify V-JEPA 2 citation} learns by predicting the embeddings of masked regions in latent space, an objective that encourages abstraction over low-level appearance. We hypothesize — and verify empirically in Sec.~\ref{sec:analysis} — that this makes V-JEPA patch tokens substantially \emph{blur-robust}: features of a blurred image stay close to those of its sharp counterpart. Such tokens are then a conditioning signal that (a)~survives the degradation, (b)~retains spatial structure that captions lose, and (c)~is available without any VLM at test time.

Our system, \textbf{ContextFlux}, keeps the entire FLUX.2 Klein backbone (transformer, VAE, text pathway) and the V-JEPA encoder frozen. A single dual-head cross-attention projector ($\sim$\tbd{}M parameters\authnote{compute from checkpoint}) maps V-JEPA tokens into the backbone's text-token stream and, through a second head, into its packed VAE-latent token stream. Notably, in our main configuration the low-quality (LQ) image latents are \emph{not} fed to the transformer at all: every bit of information about the input image reaches the backbone through projected V-JEPA tokens. Deblurring quality therefore directly measures how much restoration-relevant content the self-supervised representation carries.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.} (1)~An empirical analysis of V-JEPA representations under motion blur (spectrum, per-component invariance, and GT-vs-blur token similarity) compared against DINOv2 and CLIP; (2)~ContextFlux, a two-stage recipe — caption/latent alignment, then flow-matching fine-tuning — that conditions a fully frozen flow transformer on projected V-JEPA tokens only; (3)~a controlled comparison across conditioning encoders (V-JEPA, DINOv2, CLIP, VL-JEPA), feature depth, and feature source (degraded vs.\ oracle-sharp), against regression and LoRA-tuned diffusion baselines.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%=============================================================================

\textbf{Deblurring.} Multi-scale CNNs~\cite{nah2017deep} evolved into restoration transformers~\cite{zamir2022restormer,kong2023efficient} and simplified baselines~\cite{chen2022nafnet}. Diffusion-prior methods~\cite{lin2023diffbir,wang2024exploiting,wu2024osediff} trade distortion for perceptual quality by sampling from a pre-trained generative model conditioned on the degraded input.

\textbf{Flow matching.} Rectified-flow transformers~\cite{lipman2023flow,liu2023flow,esser2024scaling} learn a velocity field transporting noise to data; FLUX~\cite{flux2024} scales this recipe. We build on the distilled FLUX.2 Klein model, which admits guidance-free few-step sampling.

\textbf{Conditioning restoration without captions.} LucidFlux~\cite{lucidflux2025} conditions a Flux backbone for universal restoration without text captions; we go further by removing image-latent conditioning as well, isolating the representation itself as the sole carrier of image content, and by systematically comparing self-supervised encoders. Joint-embedding predictive architectures~\cite{assran2023self,bardes2025vjepa2} have not previously been used as the conditioning interface of a generative restorer; DINOv2~\cite{oquab2023dinov2} and CLIP~\cite{radford2021learning} serve as our contrastive/distillation-based comparisons.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Preliminaries}
FLUX.2 Klein is a flow-matching transformer $v_\theta$ operating on packed VAE latents. Training minimizes
\begin{equation}
\L_{\mathrm{FM}} = \mathbb{E}_{t,\x_0,\x_1}\!\left[ w(t)\, \big\| v_\theta(\x_t, t, \mathbf{c}) - (\x_0 - \x_1) \big\|^2 \right],
\label{eq:fm}
\end{equation}
with $\x_t=(1-\sigma_t)\x_1+\sigma_t\x_0$ and a discrete shifted schedule (shift $3.1582$ in training; resolution-dependent dynamic shifting at sampling). The conditioning $\mathbf{c}$ enters through a token stream processed jointly with the latent tokens; in text-to-image use, $\mathbf{c}$ holds text-encoder embeddings.

\subsection{ContextFlux conditioning}
\label{sec:contextflux}
Given LQ input $\mathbf{y}$, an optional frozen SwinIR~\cite{liang2021swinir} pre-filter produces $\tilde{\mathbf{y}}$, from which the frozen V-JEPA~2.1 encoder (ViT-L, $512^2$ input) extracts patch tokens $\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_p\times d_J}$ ($N_p{=}1024$, $d_J{=}1024$; concatenating the last $N$ blocks gives $d_J{=}1024N$).

A \emph{dual-head patch cross-attention projector} — the only trainable module — consists of a shared trunk (linear embedding to width 2048 with LayerNorm, one 8-head cross-attention layer) queried by two sets of learned query tokens:
\begin{itemize}
\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
\item \textbf{Text head:} $N_p$ queries produce tokens in the backbone's context dimension. They are \emph{appended to the frozen null-prompt embeddings}, so the transformer sees $[\,\mathbf{c}_\varnothing;\, P_{\mathrm{text}}(\mathbf{F})\,]$ in its text stream — the projected features play the role of the prompt.
\item \textbf{VAE head:} one query per packed-latent position ($1024$ at $512^2$, dim.\ $128$) produces pseudo image-latent tokens that are concatenated to the transformer's latent token sequence with their own positional ids.
\end{itemize}
Crucially, the VAE encoding of $\mathbf{y}$ itself is \emph{not} concatenated to the latent stream in our main configuration: projected V-JEPA tokens are the only pathway from the input image to the backbone. Velocity predictions are read off the noisy-latent positions only.

\subsection{Two-stage training}
\label{sec:training}
\textbf{Stage A — projector alignment.} With every network frozen, the projector is trained ($20$K steps) to map V-JEPA tokens of a source image toward two targets: (i)~the text head regresses the frozen Klein text-encoder embeddings of a Qwen3-VL caption of the clean image (MSE, weight $1.0$); (ii)~the VAE head regresses the packed VAE latents of the clean image (MSE, weight $1.0$) plus a VGG perceptual loss on the decoded prediction (weight $0.1$). The source image is the clean image by default; \emph{LQ-source} and \emph{SwinIR-pre} variants align from degraded inputs to close the train/test domain gap (Sec.~\ref{sec:ablations}).

\textbf{Stage B — flow-matching fine-tuning.} The aligned projector is hot-plugged into the frozen backbone and fine-tuned end-to-end with Eq.~\eqref{eq:fm} on blur/sharp pairs ($50$K steps, AdamW, lr $2{\times}10^{-5}$, constant schedule with $500$-step warmup, per-GPU batch $2$ on $2{\times}$A100, bf16, $512^2$ crops, gradient clip $1.0$, guidance $1.0$). Inference runs $50$ Euler steps.

\subsection{Controls and variants}
\label{sec:variants}
\textbf{Encoder swaps.} DINOv2-L ($518^2$)~\cite{oquab2023dinov2} and CLIP ViT-L/14 ($224^2$)~\cite{radford2021learning} replace V-JEPA behind a single-head (text-stream) projector. VL-JEPA~\cite{openvljepa}\authnote{verify open-vljepa citation} contributes a global semantic embedding that \emph{replaces} the null prompt, testing semantic-only conditioning.
\textbf{Image-conditioned LoRA control.} The no-context control concatenates LQ VAE latents to the latent stream (Kontext-style) and tunes the backbone with LoRA ($r{=}128$); it represents the conventional conditioning route. It is not parameter-matched to the projector and is reported as a reference point, not an ablation.
\textbf{Oracle source.} Extracting V-JEPA features from the ground-truth sharp image (unavailable at inference) upper-bounds what blur-robust features could achieve.
\textbf{Blur-map adapters.} A related variant (ContextFlux-BlurMap) injects a blur-map estimate and V-JEPA features through Coupler/PGA/PCM adapters without Stage-A alignment; we report it as an architectural alternative.

\section{V-JEPA Features Under Blur}
\label{sec:analysis}
%=============================================================================

We probe whether V-JEPA representations are usable conditioning for deblurring \emph{before} training anything. All probes use the frozen \texttt{vjepa2\_1\_vit\_large\_384} encoder.

\textbf{Blur invariance.} On matched sharp/blurred pairs from our benchmark, the mean per-patch cosine similarity between V-JEPA tokens of the sharp image and its blurred counterpart is $0.724\pm0.142$ and $0.954\pm0.049$ on two validation pairs — i.e.\ blur-induced drift is real but the representation remains strongly aligned with the sharp image. \authnote{These are 2 image pairs. Scale to 100--500 pairs for all three encoders (plan step 7) and replace this paragraph with the resulting figure: cosine-similarity distributions + per-SVD-component energy ratio $\sigma_{lq}/\sigma_{gt}$ and subspace alignment for V-JEPA vs.\ DINOv2 vs.\ CLIP, from \texttt{playground/analyze\_jepa\_svd.py}. A third legacy probe pair gave an inconsistent near-zero cosine and is excluded pending re-verification.}

\textbf{Resolution robustness.} V-JEPA 2.1 uses RoPE positional encoding, so we can run it above its $384$px pre-training resolution. On a single validation image, patch tokens extracted at $224$--$768$px keep a $0.84$--$0.91$ mean cosine similarity to the $384$px reference (Table~\ref{tab:resolution}), with stable feature norms above $384$px. We treat this as license to feed $512^2$ crops without resizing, not as evidence of equivalence. \authnote{Extend to a dataset average.}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%=============================================================================
We presented ContextFlux, which conditions a fully frozen flow-matching transformer for motion deblurring exclusively through self-supervised V-JEPA tokens mapped by a lightweight aligned projector — no captions, no VLM at test time, and in the main configuration no image-latent conditioning. Our feature analysis shows V-JEPA representations degrade gracefully under blur, motivating their use as a restoration conditioning interface, and our controlled encoder comparison quantifies this choice. \authnote{Sharpen once results land; state limitations: synthetic-blur scope, sampling cost, single dataset.}
